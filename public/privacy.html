What coolify-mcp does with your data

Last updated 20 September 2026

coolify-mcp sits between an AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor) and your own Coolify v4 instance. It holds only what is strictly necessary to operate your infrastructure on your behalf, and this page explains plainly what is stored, what is transmitted, and what the operator can access.

What is stored

Data Where & Why Kept until
Your GitHub or Google account identifier and username To bind your incoming MCP session to your enrolled Coolify instance You unlink your instance or delete your data
Your Coolify instance URL and API access token Stored in HashiCorp Vault KV v2 under users/<sha256(provider:sub)> to authenticate tool requests against your instance You press "Unlink everything" at /enroll
OAuth client registrations and authorization codes Stored hashed (SHA-256) in Vault / state file to enable standard RFC 6749 / 8414 authentication Token expiry (1 hour access, 8 hours refresh) or client revocation
Pending sign-in and enrolment tickets HMAC-signed, time-limited stateless payload carried in URL parameters 10 minutes

What the operator can see

This is a self-operated platform, not a zero-knowledge system. It is important to be completely honest about what this means:

  • Your Coolify API token is stored in HashiCorp Vault (KV v2). The application accesses Vault through a narrowly scoped Kubernetes ServiceAccount.
  • However, the platform operator has administrative / root access to the Vault cluster and the underlying Kubernetes infrastructure.
  • Technically, the platform operator can read your stored Coolify API token in plaintext.
  • We do not inspect tokens as a matter of practice, and Vault paths are keyed by SHA-256 hashes rather than plain usernames, but the storage protects the data at rest from third parties — not from the platform operator.
  • If you require zero-knowledge privacy: do not use this hosted HTTP deployment. Instead, run the open-source package locally via stdio (npx @masonator/coolify-mcp in Claude Desktop or Cursor). In local mode, your token never leaves your workstation.

What is not stored

  • Your application source code, databases, volume contents, and container disks. Those remain strictly on your own servers.
  • Your conversations with your AI assistant. Those stay between you and your AI model provider.
  • Payment information. There is no billing or payment processing on this server.

Safety and masking at the boundary

  • Masked environment variables: When listing or viewing applications and databases, environment variable values are replaced with *** by default. Revealing a specific value requires an intentional, single-variable reveal: true request.
  • No private keys: SSH private keys stored in Coolify are never returned in tool responses.
  • Prompt injection defanging: Deployment and container logs are wrapped in cryptographically random boundaries to prevent malicious log content from hijacking assistant behavior.
  • Elicitation for destructive operations: Actions that delete or stop infrastructure (such as stop_all_apps, bulk_env_update, or deleting databases) require explicit human approval with a verified blast radius.

Where data goes

  • To your Coolify instance: Every tool execution sends authenticated HTTP requests to your configured COOLIFY_BASE_URL. Requests are protected by SSRF guards and DNS pinning.
  • To your identity provider (GitHub or Google): Only during the initial sign-in handshake to verify your identity.
  • Documentation index: The search_docs tool ships with an embedded index and may check coolify.io for updates; no tokens or user details are ever sent with that check.
  • Nowhere else: No analytics, no telemetry, no third-party trackers, no advertising pixels.

Removing your data

You can revoke your credentials at any time:

  • Open /enroll and click "Unlink everything and delete the stored tokens".
  • This issues a Vault destroy call on all versions of your secret, permanently wiping the record rather than setting a soft tombstone.
  • Revocation takes effect immediately: subsequent MCP requests from any connected client will fail with 401/403.
